## Research Profile

Andrew Walsh is an independent nuclear researcher and undergraduate student at Arizona
State University pursuing dual degrees in Medicinal Biochemistry and Biophysics. His
research program — conducted entirely outside of any institutional laboratory, without
formal advising, and funded independently — spans experimental gamma spectroscopy,
radiation detection instrumentation, shielding physics, and the theoretical foundations
of targeted alpha therapy.

What distinguishes this work is not only its technical depth, but its origin: a decade
of self-directed study that preceded any formal university training. Walsh built his
research program from first principles, beginning with a single radioactive sample and
a consumer-grade Geiger counter, and expanding methodically into a functioning home
laboratory capable of producing publishable research.

## Software Development

Walsh is developing **radioisotope forensic software** designed to infer parent alpha
emitters that are undetectable via conventional gamma spectrometry. By analyzing
daughter nuclide signatures and decay chain relationships, the software reconstructs
the likely identity and activity of parent isotopes — enabling source authentication
in cases where direct measurement is not feasible. Applications include research
validation, source provenance, and applied nuclear forensics.

## Background

The foundation of this research program was not a laboratory assignment or a faculty
project — it was curiosity. A sustained interest in nuclear energy, developed over
more than a decade, eventually demanded more than passive study. After acquiring his
first radioactive sample and teaching himself the underlying physics and chemistry
through independent study, Walsh began conducting systematic experiments, upgrading
his instrumentation as his methods matured, and eventually expanding into theoretical
work in radiopharmaceuticals and nuclear medicine.

Every aspect of this program — experimental design, instrumentation, data analysis,
theoretical modeling, and manuscript preparation — has been carried out independently,
without institutional support, formal advising, or external funding. The work reflects
a research philosophy grounded in rigor, self-reliance, and a genuine commitment to
advancing the science.

## Academic & Career Trajectory

The long-term objective of this research program is to contribute meaningfully to the
development of targeted alpha therapy — a modality that demands fluency across nuclear
physics, radiochemistry, and biomedical engineering, and one that Walsh believes
represents one of the most consequential frontiers in cancer treatment.

The intended path toward that goal:

1. **M.S. in Nuclear Engineering** — to formalize and deepen expertise in radiation
   transport, detector physics, shielding design, and nuclear materials science
2. **Ph.D. in Radiopharmaceutical Chemistry** — to develop the chemical and biological
   foundations necessary for novel radiopharmaceutical design, under the mentorship
   of an advisor working at the intersection of nuclear medicine and targeted therapy
3. **Independent Research Program** — to design and build next-generation delivery
   technologies for alpha-emitting therapeutics, with a focus on recoil-tolerant
   chelation and precision targeting

The goal is not simply to study these systems — it is to build them.
